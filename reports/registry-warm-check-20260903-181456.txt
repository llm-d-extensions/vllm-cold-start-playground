Standalone probe: sanity-checking the ladder's "second boot" estimate against a real warm boot
model: Qwen3-32B, tp=1, 8192 context length  |  12 boots (3 cycles), cold and warm of each arm run back-to-back so nothing else about the cache changes
RESULT: the ladder's estimate holds up — measured warm boots land close to what "cold total minus registry-check time" predicted.

Where the numbers landed:
- baseline arm: cold total 97.4s, of which the registry check was 14.9s. Predicted warm total: 82.5s. Actually measured warm: 87.3s. Off by −4.82s, well inside that arm's own 10.01s run-to-run spread — counts as agreement.
- tuned arm (fastsafetensors + cs_fst patch): cold total 50.1s, of which the registry check was 6.94s. Predicted warm total: 43.2s. Actually measured warm: 42.3s. Off by +0.90s, inside that arm's own 1.93s spread — counts as agreement.

Key takeaways:
- Both arms agree: subtracting the registry-check time from a cold boot is a reliable way to estimate the warm-boot time, not just a rough guess.
- Per-boot detail (6 cold + 6 warm) shows the registry check collapses from ~15s / ~7s (cold) to ~10-33ms (warm) in every single boot, while imports and weight-load times stay about the same regardless of registry cache state — confirming the registry check is the only phase this cache affects.
