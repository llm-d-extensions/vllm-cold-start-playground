Key takeaways:
- The last of the helper processes didn't even start until 38.8s in, so all of its work (imports, GPU setup) happens after that point — running things in parallel instead of one-after-another is pure upside.
- Three chunks of time have no finer breakdown captured in this trace (would need more instrumentation to see what's happening inside): the weight-loading call itself (30.6s), the warmup/graph-capture step (9.85s), and the KV-cache memory-sizing call (2.07s).
- Python import time adds up to 23.3s total when you sum it across the 4 separate processes involved (worst single one: 16.7s in the main server process). Only the portion actually blocking readiness counts toward the 90.6s, but this is the cost a pre-warmed or forked process would avoid.
- The container hit its CPU limit (16 cores) and got throttled for 2.83s across 11 separate moments during startup — imports, tensor-format conversion, and compilation are all CPU-hungry, so a higher CPU limit would help.
- 889ms was spent on network calls (only 64ms of that was the actual weight download; the rest was metadata lookups).
